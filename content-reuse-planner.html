      How to reuse without repeating yourself

      • Start from something worth reusing. One researched essay, report or episode with a clear point. Reuse multiplies quality; it cannot create it.
      • Cut, do not copy. Each post takes one idea, one number or one story from the original and stands on its own.
      • Change the format, not just the words. A carousel or a 60-second clip reaches people who never read long posts.
      • Point everything home. Every piece links back to the original, so the views build one asset that gets found and cited for months.
      • Views are not people. Some views are the same people seeing the idea again. That repetition is part of the value: it is how a message sticks.

      Why reuse beats volume now

      Organic reach on social platforms has fallen as feeds fill with AI-generated posts, so posting more mostly burns the team out. Reusing a strong original costs a fraction of the hours and keeps a consistent message in front of people. A small team in Dhaka cannot out-produce a large Western brand, but it can out-reuse one.       Questions

      What is content repurposing?

      Turning one strong piece, such as an essay, report, video or podcast episode, into several shorter pieces in different formats: posts, carousels, short clips and newsletter sections. Each one reaches people the original did not, reminds those it did, and points back to it.

      How many pieces can you get from one article?

      A researched article of 1,000 to 1,500 words usually gives four to eight short posts, one or two carousels or clips, and a newsletter section. Spread them over three to six weeks so each reaches people before the next.

      How do you measure the value of repurposed content?

      Add up the views each piece adds and divide all the hours spent by the total views. If the hours per 1,000 views fall compared with the original alone, reuse is paying off. Then check saves and comments, which say more about value than likes.
