What growth actually depends on in Bangladesh

In Bangladesh the binding constraint is rarely awareness. It is trust, access and the last mile: whether a rural beneficiary can cash out without friction, whether a buyer will trust a second-hand listing, whether an employer believes healthcare can be bought as a managed service. The playbooks that work here were built on thin budgets with no safety net of ad spend, and that is exactly why they travel.

  • Go-to-market and category creation. Launching in a value-driven market with narrow channels, from a flagship Samsung launch to a marketplace category built to No. 1 against the national leader.
  • Adoption at population scale. Residence-mapped campaigns, agent networks and trust design for products that have to reach a rural, low-literacy base, the way SureCash reached ten million.
  • The B2B sales engine. Turning corporate deals into a managed account system with targets and playbooks, the way Praava Health's employer base went from 342 to roughly 1,400.
  • Cross-border in both directions. Bangladeshi companies entering the United States, and US companies entering Bangladesh, with someone who has operated in both. The dual-market operator's edge.
  • Brand and communications. Brand strategy and integrated campaigns, from agency roots at Asiatic (JWT) to awareness programmes reaching ten million.
Read the system, find the lever, move the number. That is the whole method. Everything below is what it looks like when it works.

For a company in Bangladesh, you get a strategist who learned the market from the inside, at Samsung, SureCash, Praava, Ekhanei, Asiatic and ACCA, and who has since operated in the United States. For a Bangladeshi founder raising abroad, selling abroad or hiring abroad, the translation between the two markets is most of the job, and it is a translation I do in both directions. Bangladesh already made the health-tech leapfrog; the same is true of payments and commerce, and most foreign advisers have not noticed.

Questions people ask before we talk

Do you work with Bangladeshi companies from Michigan?

Yes. Most engagements run remotely, with Bangladesh mornings overlapping US evenings, and with visits to Dhaka when the work needs them. Most of my operating record was built in Dhaka.

Which Bangladeshi companies have you led growth for?

SureCash, Praava Health, Ekhanei.com, Samsung Bangladesh, Asiatic (JWT) and ACCA Bangladesh, across fintech, healthcare, e-commerce, consumer technology, advertising and professional education.

Can you help a Bangladeshi company enter the US market?

Yes. That is the reverse of the path I took myself, and the work is mostly translation: positioning, pricing, channel and trust signals that read correctly to an American buyer.

How is this different from a marketing agency in Dhaka?

An agency sells execution in a channel. I diagnose which constraint is actually holding growth back, whether that is trust, access, pricing, channel or the sales model, and build the plan and the team to move it.

What does the first month look like?

A growth diagnosis: your numbers, your funnel, five to ten customer conversations, and a written read on the single lever most likely to move the number, with a plan you can run with or without me.
