What a fractional CGO does, and how it differs from a CMO

A CMO owns marketing. A CGO owns the number. In most Bangladeshi companies growth stalls between departments: marketing brings leads that sales cannot convert, prices are set without the channel in the room, and nobody reads the data across all of it. A fractional CGO works across those lines a few days a week, with a mandate to move one agreed number, and builds the team and the weekly routine that keep moving it after the engagement ends.

  • Growth diagnosis and the one lever. Your numbers, your funnel and conversations with your customers, read until the binding constraint is clear: trust, access, price, channel or the sales model.
  • Marketing and brand. Positioning, campaigns and content that sell, built on agency roots at Grey and Asiatic (JWT).
  • Sales and key accounts. A managed account system for corporate buyers, the model that took Praava Health’s corporate clients from 342 to about 1,400.
  • Pricing and packaging. Offers people understand and trust. At Praava, organ-targeted diagnostic packages with no physician kickbacks lifted lab margin from about 30% to about 50%.
  • Channels and partnerships. Distribution through things people already use, like a doctor hotline printed on a Lifebuoy soap bar that took about 3,000 calls a day.
  • Data. Dashboards and a weekly read so decisions rest on numbers. At Praava, a BI team built inside marketing put 65 to 70% of decisions on data.
Read the system, find the lever, move the number.

When it makes sense, and when it does not

It fits a company whose product people want but whose growth has slowed or never scaled: a funded startup between first traction and its first full-time growth executive, a family business moving into digital sales, a clinic or diagnostics group that needs more patients and more of them coming back, or a foreign company entering Bangladesh. It does not fit a company looking for someone to buy media, or one with nobody inside to own the work after the handover. The first 90 days

  • Days 1 to 30: diagnose. Your numbers, your funnel, five to ten customer conversations, and a written read on the single lever most likely to move the number.
  • Days 31 to 60: build. The plan, the team’s roles, the dashboard and the weekly routine, with the first experiments in market.
  • Days 61 to 90: scale and hand over. Keep what works, cut what does not, and prepare the person who will own it next.

Questions people ask before we talk

What is a fractional Chief Growth Officer?

A senior growth leader who owns your growth number part time, usually a few days a week, across marketing, sales, pricing, channels and data, instead of as a full-time hire.

How is a fractional CGO different from a fractional CMO?

A CMO usually owns marketing. A CGO owns the result across marketing, sales, pricing and channels, which is where growth stalls in most Bangladeshi companies.

Which industries have you worked in?

Eight: telecom, logistics, advertising, fintech, e-commerce, consumer technology, education and healthcare, at Grameenphone, MGH Group, Grey, Asiatic (JWT), SureCash, Ekhanei.com, Samsung, ACCA, Praava Health and Laser Treat Group.

Do you work with healthcare companies?

Yes. Clinics, diagnostic groups and health startups are where my growth and operations record overlap: I ran Praava Health’s consumer business, was COO of Laser Treat Group, and hold an MPH and the CHES® credential.

Can you work with us from the United States?

Yes. Most of the work runs online, with Dhaka mornings overlapping Michigan evenings for weekly reviews, and visits to Dhaka when the work needs them.

How long is an engagement?

It starts with a one-month diagnosis. After that, scope and length depend on the number we are moving, and are agreed before we start.

Can we work in Bangla?

Yes. Bangla is my first language, and there is a Bangla version of this page.
