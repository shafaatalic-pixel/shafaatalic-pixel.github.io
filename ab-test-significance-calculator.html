Three ways A/B tests mislead

  • Peeking. Checking every day and stopping the moment it looks significant inflates false wins. Decide the sample size first, then wait for it.
  • Too-short tests. Run at least one full week, ideally two, so weekday and weekend behaviour are both in the data.
  • Winning the wrong metric. A higher click rate that brings lower-value customers is not a win. Check revenue per visitor too.

Questions

What confidence level should I use?

95% is the common standard. Use 90% for low-risk changes you can easily reverse, and 99% for expensive or hard-to-undo decisions.

What does statistical significance mean here?

That a difference as large as the one you saw would be unlikely if A and B actually performed the same. It does not say the lift is big enough to matter.

How is the required sample size calculated?

For a two-sided test at your chosen confidence and 80% power, using the conversion rates you entered. Smaller lifts need much larger samples.
