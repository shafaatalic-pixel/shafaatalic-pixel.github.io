      Why staffing a hotline is not simple division

      Calls do not arrive evenly. Dividing talk time by 60 minutes gives the absolute minimum, and a hotline staffed at that level will have long queues. The Erlang C model, the standard method in call-centre planning, accounts for random arrivals and tells you how many agents it takes to answer a chosen share of calls quickly.

      I used the same thinking when standing up a population-scale health hotline handling around 3,000 calls a day. A few practical lessons:

      • Plan for the busiest hour, not the daily average. Health hotlines spike after campaigns, news and outbreaks.
      • Keep occupancy below about 85%. Above that, agents have no breathing room and quality falls, which matters when callers are worried or unwell.
      • Shorter is not always better. Cutting handle time by rushing callers can push repeat calls up. Clear scripts and good triage are the real lever.

      Questions

      What is Erlang C?

      A queueing formula that estimates the probability a caller has to wait, given how many calls arrive and how long they last. It is the standard way to size call centres and hotlines.

      What is a typical service level target?

      80% of calls answered within 20 seconds is a widely used benchmark. Crisis and emergency lines usually aim higher.

      What is shrinkage?

      The share of paid time agents are not available to take calls: breaks, training, meetings, sickness and leave. 25% to 35% is common.
