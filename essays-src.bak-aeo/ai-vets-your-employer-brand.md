Traffic from AI tools to corporate careers pages surged 1,300% in six months. 38% of jobseekers under 30 now ask AI directly, "which companies offer hybrid work," "which employers have the best parental leave", before they ever open a job listing. The GEO conversation just found HR.

## The careers page has a new first visitor, and it isn't human

Generative engine optimization has mostly been discussed as a customer-brand problem: does ChatGPT recommend your product. That framing missed a bigger shift happening in parallel. A **1,300%** jump in AI-referred careers-page traffic in six months means the candidate's first real interaction with your employer brand is increasingly a summarized, synthesized answer from a model, not your careers page copy, not your Glassdoor reviews individually, but whatever the AI decided to surface from all of it. If that summary is thin, outdated, or wrong, you lose the candidate before a recruiter ever sees a resume.

> You don't get to control the first impression anymore. You only get to control what it's built from.

## I built my own practice around exactly this problem, on the customer side first

I engineered my own site to be legible to AI crawlers before "GEO" was a common term, because I run my own AI research system as part of my daily practice, roughly 155 sessions deep, with zero fabricated facts, specifically because I built verification into how the system sources and checks claims rather than trusting model output at face value. The employer-brand version of that discipline is structurally identical: current, structured, fact-checked information about your workplace, written so a model can retrieve and cite it accurately, is now a recruiting asset with the same weight as a careers-page redesign, arguably more, since the model is the layer between you and 38% of under-30 candidates before they ever click through.

## Bangladesh's youth workforce is walking straight into this shift

This isn't only a Silicon Valley or European labor-market story. Bangladesh has one of the world's largest young workforces inside a 170-million-person market, and its jobseekers, along with a sizable diaspora candidate pool evaluating opportunities remotely, are adopting AI-mediated search at the same pace as anywhere else. Employers in Dhaka's growing tech, health-tech, and finance sectors who haven't structured their employer-brand content for AI retrieval are invisible to exactly the segment of talent asking an AI assistant "which Bangladeshi companies offer real growth paths," before that candidate ever searches a job board directly.

> Employer brand used to be a page. It's now a source document for a model that speaks for you.
