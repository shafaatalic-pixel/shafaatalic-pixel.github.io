Retail media networks, Amazon, Walmart Connect, and the ad platforms built around them, are projected to hit **$203.9 billion in 2026, up 14% year-over-year**, according to Retail Media Trends 2026 data. AI-optimized targeting inside these networks is now delivering 6x the ROI of traditional ad methods. And 76% of purchases are still happening in a physical store.

## The digital budget line and the buying decision aren't the same place

Retail media's growth curve is real, and the ROI numbers behind AI-optimized targeting are genuinely impressive, 6x is not a rounding error, it's a category advantage over how ad dollars used to be spent. But growth in the digital ad line and the location where a purchase decision actually gets finalized are two different facts, and marketers keep collapsing them into one story. **76% of purchases still happen in a physical store**, which means a channel strategy built entirely around where the ad dollars are growing fastest can still miss where the sale is actually won.

> A channel can be growing fast and still not be where the decision gets made.

That gap is exactly where budgets get misallocated, not because retail media doesn't work, but because "where the growth is" quietly gets substituted for "where the customer is standing when they decide."

## The Galaxy J2 lesson: design for where the buyer actually shops

The Galaxy J2 launch strategy I ran was built for a mass-market segment, and the defining decision wasn't which digital channel had the best headline metrics, it was recognizing that this segment's actual purchase moment happened in physical retail environments, not in the channels marketers instinctively gravitate toward. Designing the launch around where the buyer really was, instead of where the industry's attention and ad spend were flowing, was the difference between a strategy that looked smart on a media plan and one that actually moved units.

Retail media in 2026 is the same tension at a bigger scale. The AI-optimized targeting inside these networks is a genuinely powerful tool, but it's a tool aimed at influencing a decision, and 76% of the time, that decision still gets finalized somewhere the retail-media dashboard doesn't fully see: the physical aisle.

## Both channels, weighted by where the decision actually lands

The right read isn't "retail media is overhyped", the 14% growth and 6x ROI are real signals worth building around. The right read is that a channel mix optimized purely for where digital ad performance is easiest to measure will systematically underweight the channel where most purchases still close. Measurement ease and purchase reality are not the same variable, and treating them as interchangeable is how budgets drift away from where customers actually are.

**Fund the channel that's growing and the channel where the sale actually happens, they are very often not the same line item.**
