Forbes' January 2026 analysis of over 300,000 LinkedIn posts confirmed three things creators have been arguing about for years, and settled all three the same way: intuition lost. Saves have overtaken likes as the top algorithmic signal, polls drive reach but actively hurt follower growth, and hashtags, the study's own phrase, "haven't worked in years."

## Three folk beliefs, three data points that kill them

Likes feel like the obvious signal because they're the most visible one, big numbers, public validation. The data says otherwise: **saves now outweigh likes in LinkedIn's distribution ranking**, because a save is a stronger behavioral signal of genuine value than a reflexive tap. Polls look like a growth hack because they spike engagement and reach, but the same study found they actively work against follower growth, likely because poll engagement doesn't convert into the kind of relationship that makes someone hit "follow." And hashtags, the oldest piece of LinkedIn folk wisdom, simply stopped mattering years before most creators noticed.

> The tactic that looks obviously right and the tactic that actually works are frequently not the same tactic.

Every one of these myths persisted because it was measurable in the wrong metric, reach or vanity engagement, while the metric that mattered, followers and saves, moved in the opposite direction the whole time.

## The used-motorbike lesson: ignore what looks right, find what the mechanism rewards

I took a used-motorbike marketplace to the #1 category position in 2.5 months, and the obvious move at the start, the one everyone on the team assumed would work, was heavier generic paid acquisition. It looked right. It moved the top-of-funnel number everyone was watching. It wasn't what the category-ranking mechanism actually rewarded. The real lever was elsewhere in the funnel, and finding it meant setting aside the intuitive tactic and testing what the system's actual scoring logic responded to instead.

The LinkedIn mythbusting data is the same discipline applied to a different platform. Likes, polls, and hashtags are the paid-acquisition equivalent here, the moves that look like growth because they produce a visible number, while the actual mechanism (saves, follower conversion, algorithmic trust) is being optimized somewhere else entirely.

## Optimize for the metric that's actually load-bearing

None of this means stop engaging people, it means stop measuring success with the metric that feels good instead of the one that's structurally tied to distribution and growth. Saves signal that content was worth returning to. That's a harder bar than a like, and it's exactly why it's the stronger ranking input.

**Chase the metric the system actually weighs, not the one that lights up first on your notifications.**
