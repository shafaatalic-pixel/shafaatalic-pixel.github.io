Entry-level graduate roles dropped 33% year-on-year in 2026. Roughly 17,000 positions are now chasing 1.2 million applicants, while 37.3% of UK workers overall say they plan to change jobs this year. The talent market is contracting at the entry point and churning everywhere else at once.

## A funnel that's collapsing at the exact wrong end

A **33%** year-on-year drop in entry-level roles isn't a soft market correction, it's a structural narrowing of the one door every career used to walk through. Set against roughly **17,000** open graduate positions and **1.2 million** applicants, the ratio isn't competitive, it's close to a lottery. Meanwhile 37.3% of the existing UK workforce plans to change jobs this year, which means the people already employed are also on the move, pulling recruiting attention away from the entry-level pool that most needs it. Employers reading this as a buyer's market are missing the second-order effect: the graduates who do land roles under this pressure will remember exactly which employers made the process humane and which ones treated 1.2 million applicants as noise to filter, and that memory becomes the employer brand five years from now.

> A market this brutal doesn't reward the employer with the most applicants. It exposes the one with the worst process.

## Where the same pressure is a different order of magnitude

Bangladesh makes this problem look different, not smaller. It's a market of roughly 170 million people with one of the world's largest youth populations, and its own well-documented graduate un- and underemployment challenge, degree holders routinely unemployed at multiples of the national rate, a mismatch between what universities produce and what the formal job market absorbs. A UK-style employer brand strategy, polish the careers page, run a graduate scheme, doesn't scale to that demographic pressure. What does is the distribution logic behind SureCash, the Bangladesh fintech platform I worked with that reached roughly 10 million unbanked people with government stipend distribution, with no bank branch required. The lesson transfers directly to talent: in a market defined by sheer population size and constrained institutional infrastructure, employer and talent brand strategy has to be built for reach at scale from day one, mobile-first, low-friction, assuming no existing branch network or campus pipeline, rather than adapted after the fact from a model built for a smaller, denser labor market.

## The employer brand that wins this decade

Whether the constraint is 1.2 million applicants for 17,000 roles or 170 million people in a labor market with limited absorption capacity, the answer is the same discipline: build for scale and dignity simultaneously, because the applicants an employer disrespects today are the market's memory of that employer tomorrow. **33%** fewer entry-level roles means every remaining one is watched more closely than it's ever been.

> In a market this tight, the process is the brand.
