Challenge

I stepped into the Graduate Hall Director role supervising nine Resident Advisors, needing to establish credibility, keep communication consistent, create psychological safety, and support staff through resident problems and personal hardship, all while learning the role myself.

Insight

Fall feedback showed my strongest asset was relational reliability: staff knew how to reach me and felt cared for. Trust creates the conditions for performance; clarity and timing convert it into execution.

Execution

Regular one-to-ones and office hours, published agendas and written guidance, support through resident conflict and personal strain, and career development in supervision. When the fall evaluation surfaced weaker areas, I responded within two days with a structured January opening plan: five workstreams with dates, owners, and deliverables. At year end I completed individual final evaluations for all nine.

Result

Seven of nine RAs responded; 132 of 140 scaled ratings were favorable (94.3%), 14 of 20 items unanimously favorable, and all respondents strongly agreed regular one-to-ones occurred. The audience-segmentation communication training I built, delivered when 44% of the team was new, contributed to 541 documented resident engagements and the 2nd-highest connection rate on campus (39.1%).

The evidence supports rapid, concrete response to feedback, not the elimination of every gap; no second identical survey was run.

Building a Resident Connections performance-recovery system

Challenge

By mid-March the formal Resident Connections field showed only 65 of 215 active residents documented, a 30.2% completion rate, with five of nine RAs below 20%. The task was to separate genuine outreach gaps from work logged in the wrong field, and build accountability without taking the work off the RAs.

Insight

Two distinct causes were in play: connection work that had not happened, and connection work recorded in General Notes instead of the formal field (one RA had notes for all 24 residents but zero formal connections). Accountability works when the manager defines the metric, diagnoses the process failure behind it, and gives each owner a realistic recovery path.

Execution

A three-page status-and-way-forward document and a seven-week plan: individualized weekly targets for the five critical cases, a clarified definition of a formal connection versus a note, a backfill rule for qualifying past conversations, Friday audits, one-to-one priority mapping, in-person re-engagement on duty rounds, and a reserved final-audit window. I corrected one roster's denominator from 21 to 17 for accuracy and updated the Complex Director after staff conversations.

Result

The end-of-cycle synthesis produced actionable community intelligence: academic stress, financial wellness, shared-space conflict, belonging, and mental-health referrals to CAPS and the Disability Resource Center.

The verified result is the baseline, the recovery system, and the intelligence it generated; the reference record does not include a final completion percentage, so none is claimed.

Challenge

Residence-life programming easily becomes activity without learning: food is bought, residents attend, success is reported as headcount. I set out to design programs that drew residents, used funds responsibly, gave staff meaningful roles, and produced observable educational outputs tied to the residential curriculum.

Insight

Participation becomes meaningful when every event asks residents to create, practice, or commit to something tangible. I built each program around an output and treated marketing as part of the design rather than a last-minute announcement.

Execution

Three programs with promotion, facilitation, check-in, and follow-up delegated to RAs: a Conflict Art Gallery (emotional literacy through art), a four-station Adulting 101 verified by a completion passport, and a Vision Board Night (SMART goals plus campus-resource navigation). Each closed with a post-program evaluation naming concrete refinements.

Result

The three programs generated 63 attendance instances on $241.89 of verified spend, $3.84 per instance, with one program delivered at $0. My supervisor independently recognized the teams for strong attendance, effective marketing, and responsible budget use.

Learning is described as staff-observed (no formal pre/post measure), and "attendance instances" is used because unique-attendee counts were not established.

Managing resident welfare through empathy, documentation, and escalation

Challenge

Welfare cases demand both humanity and discipline: protect privacy, attempt contact through appropriate channels, avoid assumptions, connect residents to resources, document only what is known, and escalate when follow-up fails or risk exceeds the role. Three cases tested different versions: an acute medical incident, an academic/wellness referral, and a mental-health concern where the resident would not engage.

Insight

The operating principle was layered, compassionate outreach, but letting risk, not convenience, determine when to escalate. Where contact was possible I moved toward an in-person conversation and resource planning; where a resident repeatedly declined, I documented observable facts honestly and returned the case to the professional team.

Execution

Email, text, door knocks, RA outreach, and scheduled meetings; in-person contact and review of CAPS, the Disability Resource Center, and the Dean of Students with continuing check-ins; CARE documentation filed; first-line follow-up delegated to the closest RA while tracking no-shows; and, in the non-engaging case, documented that direct contact had not been established and requested guidance rather than speculating.

Result

Contact and support planning were achieved where possible, one resident left with a concrete academic action plan, and the unresolved case was escalated to the CARE team. The Housing & Residence Life Director and an Assistant Director specifically recognized the thoroughness, care, and lack of delay.

Unsuccessful contact is never presented as a resolved case.

Challenge

As the GHD on duty for Area 1, the first professional-judgment tier above the Resident Advisors, I was the decision-maker residents and staff reached first during the closed hours, across a 16-hour weekday window (5 PM to 9 AM) and 24-hour weekend rotations. The job was to triage correctly, protect residents, direct paraprofessional staff, and escalate up the chain only when the issue exceeded my authority or policy required it.

Insight

The on-call load was manageable rather than merely reactive. Across 33 shifts the incidents were not random: roughly half were operational, acuity clustered from 5 PM to midnight, and the mix tracked the academic calendar. Seeing the pattern let prevention and escalation be systematized.

Execution

I documented and resolved 156 incidents over 33 shifts (avg. 4.7 per shift) and built an automated duty-scheduling system with weekly data-informed reports. I held the line at my own tier where I could, used the mandated notify-to-dispatch step for overnight maintenance, and reserved DPS and Fire & Rescue for policy-defined life-safety events: an elevator entrapment, a weapon recovered on rounds, and a personal-safety call answered within minutes. During an August fire alarm affecting the Walton/Putnam community I responded with RAs, another GHD, and DPS and co-completed the corrected after-action report.

Result

A documented, audit-ready record of live-in crisis leadership: 156 incidents resolved, 53% held at the GHD tier, and high-acuity events led calmly and by protocol. The fire after-action record captures a 7:20 PM alarm, 7:21 evacuation, 7:25 DPS arrival, and 7:50 closure.

The evidence supports coordinated response and documentation within a team structure, not sole incident command.

Challenge

Accountability in a residential complex depends on rhythm and visibility, but duty scheduling was manual and the two data streams that show whether a community is healthy, resident-connection metrics and duty-log incidents, lived apart. Chasing performance case by case does not scale.

Insight

Accountability sticks when it is systematized and visible on a cadence, not chased after the fact. If the data already exists, the job is to automate the collection and put a weekly report in front of the team.

Execution

I built an automated duty-scheduling system and a weekly reporting routine that pulled connection and duty-log metrics into one data-informed view, institutionalizing a review cadence rather than relying on memory or ad hoc check-ins.

Result

A repeatable accountability system with weekly reporting that made performance visible and gave every downstream intervention a baseline. This infrastructure underpinned the Resident Connections recovery (Case 2) and the engagement gains from the RA team (Case 1).

The system and its weekly reporting are documented; specific time-savings or efficiency figures are not claimed, because the record does not quantify them.

Challenge

In a closed building overnight, in a Michigan winter, resident safety and facilities integrity depend on catching problems early. Resident safety, facilities walk-throughs, and prevention are distinct responsibilities, and most of that work never becomes an emergency because someone caught it first.

Insight

Prevention is eyes-on and rounds-based. The on-duty professional walking the building is the control that keeps small failures, a propped door, a failing heater, a dead battery, from becoming incidents.

Execution

On rounds I identified and routed door-security and access-control failures, responded to environmental hazards including rooms dropping to 55 to 58°F, AC leaks, and dead door batteries, used the mandated notify-to-dispatch step for maintenance, and recovered a weapon found on rounds. Each was documented with an incident report.

Result

Security gaps and facilities hazards were identified and routed for correction before they escalated, and the pattern the logs revealed, spring clusters of door-security and access-control failures, makes prevention plannable rather than reactive.

Figures are counts from the duty logs; this is framed as stewardship within the on-call structure, not a separate formal walk-through program.
