Why have you worked in so many industries?

Because I follow one skill, reading people and systems, into wherever it can move a number that matters.

I started in customer service at Grameenphone, about 80,000 conversations, to learn how people actually decide. Then brand at MGH Group in logistics, and brand and digital at two of Bangladesh’s biggest advertising agencies, Grey Dhaka and Asiatic JWT, where much of the work was for FMCG brands such as Unilever’s Lux. Then fintech at SureCash, where a government stipend reached 10 million beneficiaries. E-commerce at Ekhanei, where a category reached No. 1 in two and a half months. Consumer tech at Samsung, where I ran the mobile line for Bangladesh the way an FMCG brand manager runs a brand, and the Galaxy Note9 launch ranked first globally for growth. Education at ACCA. During the pandemic I pointed all of it at healthcare, where growth also improves lives.

Eight industries in seventeen years, one job: find the number that matters, read the people and the system around it, and move it.

Can you lead growth outside healthcare?

Yes, and the record runs the other way from what people assume. My growth and outcome results came first, and most of them came outside healthcare: a No. 1 category in e-commerce in two and a half months, Samsung’s Note9 ranked first globally for growth, a fintech that reached 10 million beneficiaries, brand and digital work at two of Bangladesh’s biggest advertising agencies, Grey Dhaka and Asiatic JWT, and ACCA in education. Twelve years of that, across seven industries, before I touched public health.

The MPH and CHES came later, during the pandemic, because I wanted to point those same skills at the market where growth is hardest: regulated, evidence-bound, and built on trust. Health education is behaviour change at scale, which is what marketing is when it is done honestly. If a method works there, it works in SaaS, retail or finance with fewer constraints.

So read the MPH as an addition, not a pivot. What I bring to any industry is the same: find the number that matters, read the people and the system around it, and move it. What is your proof in health, for business growth and population outcomes?

Both, and they connect. On the business side: at Praava Health I directed growth through a multi-year hypergrowth run, around 45 percent overall and 57 percent business-to-consumer compound annual growth from 2021 to 2023, built a corporate sales engine from zero that took corporate clients from 342 to about 1,400, and lifted winter footfall by roughly 190 percent. At LaserTreat I ran operations as COO for a multi-entity clinical group.

On the population side: a doctor hotline printed on a Lifebuoy soap bar took 3,000 calls a day, a government stipend reached 10 million beneficiaries through SureCash, an ethical diagnostics model moved lab margin from about 30 to 50 percent without kickbacks, and a campus harm-reduction campaign at EMU was designed and evaluated on behavioural science.

That combination is why I founded HSREP, a two-country platform on health system resilience, and why I hold the MPH and CHES. If your problem is reach, trust or adherence at scale, that is the work.

I am the right call when there is a number that is not moving: acquisition cost, retention, reach, coverage, adherence, or an operating cost that has outrun revenue. The team needs both the diagnosis and the lever, in a US or Bangladesh context.

How would you grow a new clinic in Dhaka, or a cash-pay clinic in the US?

I would lead with the lab, not the doctor visit. In a cash-pay market, price and trust decide everything, and a test wins on both: it is cheaper than a full consultation, concrete, and gives the patient a result they own and can act on. So design the test as the product: fast, transparent, trustworthy, and connected to a clear next step. At Praava, when COVID hit, we stood up 6-hour traveler testing and home sample collection, and that trust compounded into roughly 45% overall and about 57% B2C CAGR over multiple years. The US is arriving at the same model through at-home and direct-to-consumer diagnostics.

What is HSREP?

HSREP, Health System Resilience & Economic Protection, is an independent platform I founded that publishes health-system arguments and turns them into advocacy professionals can act on. Its argument is that health protection is economic infrastructure, not a residual budget line. It grew out of an MPH capstone at Eastern Michigan University and now publishes independently, with sources, methods and limitations disclosed on every substantive piece. Season 1 was an eight-week, two-country campaign of nine published pieces that generated 57,274 gross platform-reported views and impressions at $0 ad spend. Those are impressions, not people reached. It also carries the Prevention Adoption Initiative, a proposed colorectal-screening completion pilot.

What have you written about vapes and nicotine pouches?

In my essay The quiet epidemic (September 2026), I argue that tobacco and the new nicotine are one epidemic wearing two faces. In the US the front line has moved to youth vaping: around 2 million middle- and high-schoolers still use a tobacco product, and about 76% of teens who vape reach for it within 30 minutes of waking. Nicotine pouches are the newest form, used by 1.7% of US middle- and high-schoolers in 2025, against 5.2% for e-cigarettes. Shame never wins this fight. The exit has to be easier than the habit: cessation support, dependence screening and youth campaigns on the platforms kids use.

How do you actually run your work with AI?

I frame the problem, orchestrate the models, challenge the output, and tie the work to a number. My framework has five stages: understand the system, structure the intelligence, orchestrate the AI, challenge and verify, then implement and measure. Each step goes to the right model, a human gate sits before anything public, and logs show what happened. I design and direct AI-assisted builds; I am not a machine-learning engineer training models. I also test my site assistant in public: in the Proof Lab, its final round passed 75 of 75 answers on 23 October 2026, with every failure and fix shown.
