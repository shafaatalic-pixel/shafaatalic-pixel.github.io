The idea behind ESOV

Brands whose share of category advertising (share of voice) is higher than their share of the market tend to grow; brands below it tend to shrink. The commonly quoted rule of thumb from IPA effectiveness research is about half a share point of growth a year for every 10 points of excess share of voice. It varies by category, by brand size and, above all, by how good the work is.

  • Small brands can usually afford a higher ESOV relative to their size; market leaders often grow with less.
  • Estimate competitor spend honestly. Use media monitoring data or a reasoned range, and test both ends.

Questions

What is share of voice?

Your share of all advertising in your category, usually measured by spend or impressions. If the category spends 10 million and you spend 2 million, your share of voice is 20%.

What is excess share of voice (ESOV)?

Share of voice minus share of market. Positive ESOV is associated with market share growth; negative ESOV with decline.

How accurate is the 0.5 points per 10 ESOV rule?

It is an average from effectiveness studies, not a law. Use it to size ambition and budgets, then track your own results.
