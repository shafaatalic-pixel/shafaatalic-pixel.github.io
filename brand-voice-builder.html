Voice stays, tone flexes

Your brand voice is its personality, and it should stay consistent everywhere. Tone is how that voice adapts to the moment: lighter on social, steadier in a complaint reply, calmer in a health message. A good guide gives writers a few clear principles and examples, not a long list of rules.

  • Only strong positions become rules. Sliders near the middle mean "flex as needed", so the guide leaves them out.
  • Write three sample lines (a headline, an error message, an apology) to test the guide before you roll it out.

Questions

What is the difference between brand voice and tone?

Voice is the consistent personality behind everything a brand writes. Tone is how that voice adjusts to the situation and the reader's mood.

How do I define a brand's tone of voice?

Place the brand on a few spectrums such as formal to casual and serious to playful, turn the strong positions into do and avoid principles, then test them on real copy.

How many principles should a voice guide have?

Three to five is usually enough to remember and apply.
