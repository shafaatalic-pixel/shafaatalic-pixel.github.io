Margin and markup are not the same

Margin is profit as a share of the selling price. Markup is profit as a share of cost. The same product always has a higher markup than margin, which is why confusing them leads to under-pricing. Retailers and distributors often quote one while suppliers think in the other, so agree which one you mean.

Questions

What is the difference between margin and markup?

Margin divides profit by the selling price; markup divides profit by cost. A product costing 40 and selling for 60 has a 33% margin and a 50% markup.

How do I price for a target margin?

Divide cost by one minus the target margin. For a 45% margin on a cost of 40, the price is 40 ÷ 0.55 = 72.73.

Which costs should I include?

For unit margin, include every cost that rises with each sale: product, packaging, delivery and payment fees.
