Most population-health failures are distribution failures

The intervention usually exists. What is missing is the route to the household: a channel that reaches people where they already are, a financing arrangement that does not push them into poverty, and a prevention programme designed for how people actually live rather than how a guideline assumes they do. A hotline on a bar of soap is the shortest version of that argument, and it was a real programme.

  • Access and distribution strategy. Partnership channels that put a health service inside something people already buy or use, the way a soap brand became a nationwide patient-acquisition channel.
  • Prevention programme design. Evidence-based programmes for outcomes where elimination is unrealistic, from campus harm reduction to the first 1,000 days.
  • Health financing and household protection. The case for prevention and resilience as investment rather than cost, and the mechanisms that stop a health shock becoming a financial one. This is HSREP's standing focus.
  • Surveillance and data partnerships. Population-scale access programmes that also produce public-health surveillance data, in partnership with the Directorate General of Health Services.
  • Health-system resilience planning. What a system needs before the next shock, not after. The workforce crisis and the community-health-worker answer.
A health system is measured by what happens to the household that gets sick, not by what happens inside the hospital.

HSREP: resilience and economic protection as one problem

HSREP is the platform I founded to work on health-system resilience and economic protection together, because in most of the world they are the same problem: a health shock is a financial shock, and a system that cannot protect households from one cannot protect them from the other. Its standing lenses are household financial protection, system resilience and preparedness, health financing as investment, and prevention and adoption. The consulting work on this page and the platform inform each other.

Who this is for, and who it is not

It is for health systems and ministries, NGOs and development programmes, funders who need an operator's read on a population-health investment, and universities and research groups that need a practitioner on the team, in Bangladesh and the United States. It is not for clinical epidemiology or modelling; I work alongside epidemiologists on what the numbers mean for the programme.

Questions people ask before we talk

How is population health different from health communication?

Health communication changes what people believe and do. Population health changes what a system does for a whole population: access, financing, prevention and resilience. I work on both, and they usually need each other.

What is HSREP?

Health System Resilience and Economic Protection: a platform I founded on the link between health shocks and household finances, with a public prevention-adoption initiative and a research and advocacy programme.

Do you work with funders and NGOs?

Yes. Most population-scale work involves a funder, an implementing partner and a public agency, and much of the job is making those three pull in the same direction.

Do you work in both Bangladesh and the United States?

Yes. The population-scale programmes are from Bangladesh; my public-health training and the campus programme are from Michigan; the resilience and financing work spans both.

What does the first month look like?

A systems diagnosis: who is not being reached, why, what it costs the household when they are not, and a written read on the single point in the system where a change would reach the most people.
