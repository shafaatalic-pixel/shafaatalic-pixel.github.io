      Score it together

      An idea scorecard is most useful as a conversation tool: ask three or four people to score independently, then discuss the tests where scores differ most. That is usually where the real risk sits. A high score does not guarantee success, but a very low score on any single test is a reliable warning.

      Questions

      How do you evaluate a marketing idea?

      Against a few consistent tests: is it distinctive, relevant, on strategy, makeable, measurable, does it invite participation and can it scale? Scoring each from 1 to 5 makes the discussion concrete.

      What score is good enough?

      Around 80 out of 100 suggests a strong idea; 60 to 80 is promising but needs work. Any single test scored 1 is worth fixing regardless of the total.

      Can I change the tests?

      Yes. These seven cover most briefs, but some projects need others, such as cost per participant or cultural fit across markets.
