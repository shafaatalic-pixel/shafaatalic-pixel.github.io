        What makes an objective SMART

        • Specific. One population, one behaviour or outcome. "Improve health" is a goal, not an objective.
        • Measurable. A baseline and a target, in the same unit, from a named data source.
        • Achievable. Ambitious but credible given the budget, time and evidence from similar programs.
        • Relevant. Tied to the intervention, so a change can reasonably be credited to the work.
        • Time-bound. A date by which it will be measured.

        Behaviour and outcome objectives like these sit under a broader goal, and above process objectives such as "train 120 health workers by March".

        Questions

        What is a SMART objective in public health?

        An objective that is Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant and Time-bound. It states who will change what, by how much, by when, and how it will be measured.

        What is the difference between a goal and an objective?

        A goal is the broad, long-term change you want. Objectives are the measurable steps that show progress towards it.

        What if I do not have a baseline?

        Plan a baseline measurement first, or use the most recent data available for a similar population, and say so in the objective.
