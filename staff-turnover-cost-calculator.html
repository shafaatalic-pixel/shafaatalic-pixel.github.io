      Where the cost of a leaver hides

      • Empty days are rarely free. Someone covers the shift with overtime or an agency, or the work simply does not get done: a patient waits, a household is not visited, a sale is lost.
      • Hiring and training are the visible part. Ads, agency fees, interview hours and orientation show up in a budget line, so they get most of the attention.
      • Ramp-up is the part nobody books. A new nurse or analyst needs months to work at full speed. This tool counts half their pay as lost output during that time.
      • Count the yearly bill, not the single exit. One exit looks small. Multiply by your turnover rate and headcount and it becomes a line the board will read.

      Hospitals show the scale

      US hospital nurse turnover was 17.6% in 2025, each staff nurse who left cost about $60,090, and the average hospital lost about $5.19 million a year to it. Each point of turnover was worth about $295,000 a year. The nurse example here is set to land close to those figures. In Bangladesh the same arithmetic applies to lab technologists, garment operators and field sales teams, where pay is lower but turnover is often higher.       Questions

      How much does it cost to replace an employee?

      Gallup estimates one-half to two times the employee's annual salary. The cost adds up from the days the post stays empty, hiring, training and the months a new hire needs to get up to speed. For a US hospital staff nurse, the 2026 NSI report puts it at about $60,090.

      How do you calculate the cost of staff turnover?

      Work out the cost of one leaver: empty working days times daily pay times the cost of covering them, plus hiring and training costs, plus ramp-up months times monthly pay times the share of output lost. Then multiply by headcount and your annual turnover rate.

      What does a one-point cut in turnover save?

      Multiply 1% of your headcount by the cost of each leaver. For a hospital with 500 staff nurses at about $60,000 each, that is about $300,000 a year, close to the $295,000 the NSI report gives for the average hospital.
